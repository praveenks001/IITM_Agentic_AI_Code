Question:
What is the difference between Open positions and closed positions?

Answer:
Open positions refer to any trading positions or transactions that have not yet been closed. This means that the trader still holds the position and is exposed to market fluctuations. For example, if a trader has bought a stock (open long position) and has not sold it yet, or if they have sold a stock short and have not yet bought it back, these are considered open positions.

In contrast, closed positions are those that have been finalized or settled. This occurs when a trader executes a transaction that offsets their open position, effectively locking in any profits or losses. For instance, if the trader who bought the stock sells it, or if the trader who sold short buys it back, those transactions would close their respective positions.

In summary, the key difference is that open positions are still active and subject to market changes, while closed positions have been completed and are no longer subject to market risk.
